MicroDuck · battery backpack · build guide for teammates

Build the battery backpack

A small printed box on the duck's back that holds a 2S LiPo, an on/off switch and a 5 V converter. It plugs into the robot with two connectors, so we can swap between the desk supply and the battery in under a minute.

You will make four short leads (S, C, D, E), put them with the battery into the printed box, and check every plug with a multimeter. Nothing on the robot itself gets cut, soldered or rewired.

Before you startA LiPo can catch fire if its leads touch or the pack is crushed. Keep the battery unplugged and away from the iron until section 7, and never solder anything that is connected to it.

01The robot side: what you plug into

The robot has exactly two power leads, and they stay as they are: the same wires along the same route. Only their free ends, behind the electronics pack, carry a plug half. Shawn fits those two plugs once. Every power source then carries the matching sockets: your battery backpack, and the desk box through two short adapter leads screwed into its converters. Swapping is two plugs, nothing else.

Why plugs and not screw terminals: a swap with screw terminals means loosening four wires and clamping them somewhere else, and the 5 V lead could end up under a battery-voltage screw. XT30 and MR30 have different shapes and only fit one way round, so battery voltage can never reach the 5 V computer and the polarity cannot be reversed.

Your job ends at the two sockets (plugs 3 and 4). You do not touch the robot or the desk box.

03Mark polarity before you solder anything

XT30 and MR30 housings have no reliable + marking you can see while soldering. So mark every plug with a paint dot on its + side first. Then the rule is simple: red wire goes into the cup under the dot, black into the other.

  1. XT30 males (for lead C and the battery end of S): plug each onto the battery's XT30. Put a dot on the shell on the same side as the battery's red wire. Unplug.
  2. XT30 females (for lead D and the far end of S): plug each onto a male you just marked. Dot the side facing the male's dot.
  3. MR30 female (lead E): plug it onto the robot's MR30 male and dot the side facing its dot. If the robot's plug is not fitted yet, dot one side of a spare MR30 male from the same bag, use it as the reference, and give it to Shawn for the robot lead. The middle contact stays empty.
  4. Plug every pair together once more and look: both dots on the same side.

04Solder a wire into a plug cup

Every plug in this build is soldered the same way. The back of each contact is a small open cup. A good joint fills the cup, holds the wire when you tug it, and has no solder bridging to the neighbouring cup.

Quick test after every plugSet the meter to continuity (beep). Touch the two contacts of the same plug: it must stay silent. A beep means solder bridged the two cups.

05Build the switch lead S

The switch interrupts only the red wire. The black wire runs straight from one plug to the other and never touches the switch. The battery end gets an XT30 male (it mates the battery's female plug 1); the other end gets an XT30 female (plug 2, for lead C).

  1. Cut: two red pieces of 6 cm, one black piece of 12 cm. Strip 5 mm at each end, twist, tin.
  2. Slide on heat shrink first: a 15 mm piece of 3 mm tube on each red wire (push it to the middle, away from the heat), and the 10 mm XT30 pieces on all wire ends that go into plugs.
  3. Solder the lugs: clamp the switch lugs up. Hook one red wire through each lug hole, bend it back, and heat for 2–3 seconds while feeding solder. Then stop. The switch body is plastic; heating too long softens it and the rocker sticks.
  4. Cover the lugs: once cool, slide each 3 mm tube over its lug and shrink it. The lugs are close together, so each needs its own tube.
  5. Male XT30 (battery end): red wire from lug 1 into the dotted cup, one end of the black into the other cup.
  6. Female XT30 (plug 2 end): red wire from lug 2 into the dotted cup, the other end of the black into the other cup. Finish both plugs as in section 4.

06Solder leads C, D and E to the DFR1202

Three pairs land on three pads on the board's edge. Leads C (from the switch) and D (to the servos) share the VIN and GND pads: the servo current passes through those two joints, so make them solid. Lead E takes the 5 V from VOUT.

  5. VOUT: E red.
  6. Pull test and short test. Once cool, tug each wire: nothing moves. Continuity mode: VIN–GND and VOUT–GND must not beep steadily (a short chirp is the input capacitor charging, that is fine). VIN–VOUT must not beep.
Why VIN and GND matter mostAll servo current flows through those two joints: about 1 A while walking and around 8 A for a fraction of a second when the duck stands up. A dull, grainy joint will warm up. Make them shiny and full.

07Meter checks before anything touches the robot

Do this with the battery and the multimeter only, on the bench, outside the box. Red probe in the dotted side, black probe in the other side.

  1. No battery, continuity mode: across plug 3 (D) and across plug 4 (E): no steady beep.
  2. Switch OFF. Plug the battery into S (plug 1) and S into C (plug 2). DC volts: plug 3 reads 0 V.
  3. Switch ON. Read plug 3 and plug 4. Compare with the simulator below.

08Assemble the backpack

Order matters: the switch goes in from below, the converter from behind, and the leads must already be soldered because the plugs pass through holes in the print.

Open the 3D steps
  1. Switch into the floor, rocker down. Hold the frame upside down. Feed both XT30 plugs of S up through the 12.3 × 27.5 mm floor hole, then push the switch body after them until both snap wings click over the panel. Press on the body, not the rocker. The rocker ends flush in its pocket.
  2. Foam on the inductor. Stick the 2 mm pad on the DFR1202's big grey inductor. It rests against the battery later and stops rattling.
  3. Plugs 3 and 4 out the front window. From inside, push D's XT30 and E's MR30 out through the flat window in the front plate, about 4 cm. MR30 dot facing up.
  4. Clip the DFR1202 in. Pads down, components towards you, VIN end on the stop side. Slide it straight forward from the open back until it sits on the two ribs, then press its long edges past the flexible lips until they click. Do not over-bend the lips.
  5. Plug C into S (plug 2) and tuck it into the space on the robot's left side of the battery bay. It stays plugged; it only exists so the switch can be replaced.

09Mount it and swap power

Do the first mount together with Shawn. After that, a swap takes about a minute.

  1. Shut the A7Z down (sudo shutdown -h now), wait 10–15 s, then unplug the desk supply's USB-C.
  2. Take the desk box off. Remove its cap (four M2 × 10). Lift the top converter off its two lower M2 × 4 and let it hang on its wires, so you can reach the upper cover screws. Remove all four cover screws (M2 × 6, keep them), pull the box back about 4 cm and unplug its two adapter sockets from the robot's XT30 and MR30.
  3. Plug in the backpack about 4 cm behind the robot: plug 3 onto the robot's XT30, plug 4 onto its MR30. Stand both plugs in the channel.
  4. Fix the frame with four M2 × 6: the upper two through the empty battery bay, the lower two from behind under the box floor. Start all four, then tighten evenly.
  5. Battery in. Switch off. Plug the battery into plug 1 outside the box, stand it upright with leads at the top on the robot's right side, slide it in against the foam. Nothing under the battery.
  6. Cap on. 1 mm foam inside the cap between its two fences, push it straight on, three M2 × 8 from the back.
  7. Power on by pressing the rocker from below, with someone holding the robot. It may wake up lying on its side; that is normal.
Two rules for every swapSwap only with the switch off and the A7Z shut down. Never connect the desk supply and the battery to the robot at the same time.

10LiPo safety and charging

  • Charge off the robot, in the LiPo bag, with someone watchingUse a balance charger on the white JST-XH balance plug. 0.6–0.65 A (1C) is the safe rate for this 650 mAh pack.
  • New packs arrive at storage charge (~3.8 V per cell)Charge fully before the first run. Return unused packs to storage charge within a few days.
  • Stop at 6.6 VThe robot's software warns at 7.0 V and stops at 6.6 V (3.3 V per cell). Going lower damages the pack.
  • Retire a pack that puffs, runs hot, or shows more than 0.05 V between cellsCheck the cells with the LiPo checker after charging.
  • Power off in this orderA7Z shutdown, wait 10–15 s, hold the robot, then switch off. The servos go limp the instant the switch opens.
  • Never plug a USB-C supply into the A7Z while the backpack powers itThe header 5 V and the USB-C port are the same wire on that board.
