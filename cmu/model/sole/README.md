# Foot sole material, 2026-10-18

Official reference: Pollen's maintainer notes that the real Microduck has a grippy polyurethane (PU) sole, while training randomised foot friction mu 0.7-1.3 (`microduck_rl/scripts/infer_policy.py`, `--foot-friction` help, commit bfc0ed4, 2026-08-19). The runtime repo (pollen-robotics/microduck, read 2026-10-18) names no sole material.

Our feet ran on sandpaper over 0.5 mm 3M foam tape. RoboClub had only a 2 mm rubber roll. Withdrawn: the 0.8 mm 60A silicone sheet (Amazon B0B4X47N19, $14.99) was removed from the cart at the user's word; nothing ordered. Free routes first: sandpaper stuck directly with thin double-sided tape (no foam layer), or a discarded bicycle inner tube (butyl, about 1 mm) ([offers](offers.json), [authorization](authorization.json)).

Cutting: the foot bottom is flat over about 39 x 27 mm and curves up 1 mm at 3.5 mm and 2 mm at 5 mm past that edge. In the sim walk at 0.25 m/s about 90 % of floor contacts sit on that curve (foot tilt 6-10 deg), so the sheet covers it: trace the foot outline on the backing paper, cut 2 mm inside the line (about 50 x 38 mm), cut a small V notch at each corner, and press the sheet over the curve from the centre outward.

Contact study: `~/.venvs/microduck-dyn/bin/python sole_contact_sim.py out.json --model asbuilt --volt 7.0 --drop 0.04 --vx 0.25 --walk-at 4 --walk-s 6` (walk61, as-built model, real-condition flags). Standing: all contacts on the flat. Walking: contact height above the flat median 0.5 mm, 90th percentile 1.1 mm, max 1.6 mm.

## Sole stack thickness (sim sweep, 2026-10-19)

`sole_thickness_sim.py` inflates the two foot collision meshes by the stack thickness (MuJoCo geom margin, rigid; compliance not modelled) and runs walk61 at 0.25 m/s for 8 s on the as-built model with the real-condition flags (`--volt 7.0 --drop 0.04`). Results in `sweep/summary.json` (27 runs):

- 0, 1, 1.5, 2, 3 and 5 mm, seeds 0-2: no fall in any run; max tilt 5.3-6.7 deg, distance 0.83-0.90 m, max action 0.85-0.91 rad, all within the seed-to-seed spread at 0 mm. Swing-foot peak height is 6 mm (median) and 13.5 mm (90th pct) at every thickness: both feet carry the same sole, so the stance leg lifts the body by exactly what the swing sole adds, and clearance does not change.
- Head board sensitivity: the as-built manifest still carries the Pi Zero W (add-pi, 9.3 g) although the A7Z is fitted since 2026-10-17. Radxa publishes no mass for the Cubie A7Z (product brief section 7 gives dimensions only; docs and ameridroid pages likewise, read 2026-10-19), so the A7Z is bounded as 15 g and 20 g at the Pi position (`--head-swap --a7z-g`): 0, 1 and 2 mm soles still never fall, tilt 6.2-6.7 deg. Open item: weigh the A7Z (with header) when the head is next open and replace add-pi in `asbuilt-mass-manifest.json`.
- The 0.15 m/s rows stand in place (walk61 does not step at that command) and test nothing.

Conclusion: a 1 mm inner tube plus thin tape needs no foot reprint. Up to 5 mm the sim shows no change, so the practical limit is set by the wrap, not by walking: a sheet thicker than about 1.5 mm will not follow the 7-8 mm edge radius and lifts at the edges. Tape: use a thin PET-carrier or transfer double-sided tape (0.05-0.2 mm); the 0.5 mm foam tape used until 2026-10-18 adds a layer whose shear compliance is 10-50 x that of the rubber (acrylic foam G about 0.1 MPa, 50A butyl about 0.8 MPa) and whose edges peel.

Paper print scale (2026-10-21): CampusPrinting (`ipps://printing.andrew.cmu.edu:9164/printers/CampusPrinting`, PaperCut AirPrint) printed the 50 mm bar at 48 mm on Letter, and Chrome's Scale Default and Custom 100 gave the same result. The PDF bar is 50.00 mm on a Letter page, and the Mac's own print filter (`cupsfilter` with the queue's PPD) passes it at 50.00 mm with or without `print-scaling`/`fit-to-page` options, so the shrink happens on the server or printer side: the queue's imageable area is 4 mm (11.34 pt) in from every edge, and fitting the full page into it scales by (612 - 22.68) / 612 = 96.3 %, i.e. 50 mm -> 48.1 mm, matching the measurement. The server does not list `print-scaling` among its attributes. Compensation on this queue: Chrome Scale Custom 104 (104 x 0.963 = 1.0015, 50.1 mm), not yet verified on paper. The PDF carries `/PrintScaling /None` for viewers that honour it. Paper only; this says nothing about 3D-print dimensions. The template outline still matches the feet on the robot: the V1.22 slip-fit feet (`guide/print/foot-slipfit-20261019/`) match `community-44`/`-46` to under 0.001 mm over the bottom 4 mm.

Template (superseded by the double cut in the press mold process below): `sole_template.py` writes `sole_template.pdf` (US Letter, 1:1, 50 mm check bar, two per foot): the printed foot bottom developed to 4.5 mm above the flat (the press mold glue line; sim contacts reach 1.6 mm), with a 2.6 mm dart at each corner (about 2.5 mm of excess per doubly curved corner at that height).

Official sole (read 2026-10-19): Open Duck Mini v2, the open design the MicroDuck descends from, prints `foot_bottom_tpu.stl` in TPU at 40 % infill and screws it to `foot_bottom_pla.stl` with two M3x6 (apirrone/Open_Duck_Mini v2 print guide and assembly guide); the BOM lists no separate rubber or foam. Pollen's public CAD shows the sole as a separate yellow layer under the foot body (teardown article, 2026); Pollen's training script calls the real sole "grippy polyurethane" (microduck_rl infer_policy.py). A TPU print is a polyurethane; whether Pollen's kit part is printed or moulded is not published. Printed TPU is slippery when hard (95A) and printed smooth; the grip of a PU sole comes from low hardness (30-60A) and a matte surface, which is why cast PU (two-part urethane rubber poured in a printed mould) or sheet rubber grips better than a 95A print.

## Hot-glue sole cast in a printed mold (2026-10-19)

**Dropped 2026-10-19, before any cast.** A V1.21 PLA mold warped under the heat gun (step 4 below was wrong: no printed plastic survives a heat gun), the glue has to be pressed within seconds without it, and casting a room-temperature sole is not available (Shawn). The soles go to bike inner tube on thin PET or transfer tape, cut with `sole_template.pdf`; see the Conclusion of the thickness sweep above. The V1.23 flat-floor molds (`guide/print/release-V1.23/`, withdrawn) and the notes below are kept as the record.

RoboClub's suggestion: coat the foot bottom with hot glue. Free-hand coats grip well but the face is uneven. `hotglue_mold.py` builds a mold from each foot STL (left `community-44`, right `community-46`):

- Cavity floor: one plane 1.0 mm below the foot's flat, and on the curve the foot bottom offset outward by 1.0 mm along the normal, lowered by 0.12 mm so the two meet without a step, up to 3.0 mm above the flat (sim contacts reach 1.6 mm). Coat 1.00-1.12 mm. The flat of the printed foot varies by 0.1 mm; the V1.21 floor copied that and printed as irregular one-layer islands (Shawn's photo IMG_7040, 2026-10-19), which a cast sole would carry.
- Outside the cavity the mold top is the plane 3.0 mm up. It is also the depth stop: the foot's curved edge lands on the cavity rim all round (within 0.05 mm in 24 of 36 sectors, within 0.3 mm in all), so it cannot rock and the coat is set by the rim. The rim meets the foot at about 45 deg, so a rim printed 0.1 mm wider or narrower moves the coat 0.1 mm. The V1.21 depth pins (1.2 mm conical tops) broke off at a touch in PLA and are gone. Animation: `rim-stop.gif` (`rim_stop_animation.py`).
- Why the coat stops at 3 mm: the foot has to tilt more than 45 deg before the floor touches it 3 mm up its edge (2.2-2.4 mm at 45 deg, 1.1 mm at 30 deg, 0.2 mm at 10 deg; computed from the foot STL), and the walking sim never touches above 1.6 mm.
- Eight vents lead excess glue to a moat; four tabs 0.4 mm outside the foot outline, up to 12 mm, centre the foot.
- A raised L or R (0.6 mm, 6.5 mm tall) sits on a 7 mm strip at the heel end; the top of the letter points to the toe. The two molds are mirror images that differ by under 0.5 mm in the cavity, so the eye cannot tell them apart, and a foot pressed into the wrong one rides 0.45 mm high on the seal edge (thicker, uneven coat, glue leaks at the gap). - Check (`hotglue-mold-check.json`): no overlap between the seated foot and the mold, coat 1.00-1.12 mm along the normal (1st-99th pct), one floor level over the flat, coat volume 2.1 cm3 per foot, cavity 1859 mm2, rim contact sectors.
- Print: release V1.23 (`guide/print/release-V1.23/`, release pipeline v2) on the Black plate with the two ankle sleeves; cavity up, 0.12 mm layers, top ironing, seam at the back, 6 top layers, 40 % infill, no support, no brim. Print times and grams are in the release files' names and reviews. The V1.21 molds (printed black 2026-10-19) are replaced; their meshes are in `guide/print/release-V1.23/retired/`. The slip-fit feet (`guide/print/foot-slipfit-20261019/`) keep the sole unchanged below 4 mm, so these molds fit them.

Process:
0. Dry fit, once per foot, before any glue: set the clean foot into its mold. Its curved edge rests on the cavity rim all round and it must not rock. With a sharp pencil, draw a short line on the foot's side at the top edge of each of the four tabs. These four lines are the depth gauge: seated foot = every line level with its tab top.
1. Peel the old glue off. Sand the foot bottom with the sandpaper, wipe with alcohol. Nothing oily on the foot.
2. Wipe a thin film of release on the mold cavity and top (cooking oil or petroleum jelly; silicone spray if at hand), wipe off the excess so no pools stay. A paper or PTFE liner wrinkles in the curved cavity and prints the wrinkles into the sole.
3. One foot at a time, with the foot in your other hand. Fill the cavity from the centre outward with the glue gun in one continuous pass, about 2.5 cm3 (6 cm of 11 mm stick), slightly domed.
4. No heat gun, ever, on the mold or the foot. Its air is far hotter than any printed plastic takes: PLA softens at about 60 C, and PETG, ABS and ASA at 80-105 C would deform the same way (a V1.21 PLA mold warped under it, Shawn 2026-10-19). The glue has to stay liquid on its own, so step 5 follows step 3 at once. If the surface has skinned before the foot goes in, let it cool, peel the glue out and fill again.
5. Within about 10 seconds of the last glue, press the foot straight down between the tabs until its edge stops on the rim; hold one minute. Check the four pencil lines: each must be level with its tab top. A line above its tab means the foot rides high on that side (glue too cold or too much): let it cool, peel and recast. Glue should come out of the vents, not as a bead around the whole edge.
6. Cool 5 minutes (2 minutes in a freezer helps release), lift the foot out, trim the vent flash and the edge with a knife. Check the edge of the coat: on a seated foot it ends in a clean line all round at the rim. A thick bead of glue on the foot's side above that line means the foot rode high there; peel and recast.

If a mold loses its shape, print it again (V1.23 Black plate).

Release, by construction and print: the cavity is a height field seen from above, so it has no undercut and the foot lifts straight up (the tabs keep it straight; a tilted pull makes the layer steps catch). The coat is on the convex foot, so as the glue cools and shrinks it pulls toward the foot and away from the mold; cooling fully (freezer) helps. Printed cavity up, 0.12 mm layers with top ironing, seam at the back. Hot glue bonds to PLA, and a 190 C high-temperature gun can soften the PLA skin (PLA melts about 150-160 C), so use the release agent every cast. A glue gun only touches the cavity skin briefly; a heat gun heats the whole mold and is never used.

## Inner tube sole in a press mold (2026-10-24)

Thin tape gave way to E6000 because the contact cement had expired. E6000 grabs slowly, so on the rounded edge the cut tube springs back and lifts. A cut tube also keeps its curl. `press_mold.py` builds a mold and a screw-down lid for each foot, so the glued tube is held on the curve until it cures. Nothing else is needed: no weights and no clamps (Shawn). Release V1.34 (`guide/print/release-V1.34/`): one Black plate with both molds and both lids, fast tool process (0.28 mm layers, 3 walls, 10 % infill, no support; X1C 2 h, Elegoo 2 h 13; the stock Black profile took 6 h).

Tube (calipers, Shawn): 0.7 mm between the moulded ribs on the outer face, 0.8 mm on a rib. The cavity offset is 0.70 mm with the ribs on the floor side, where they meet the cavity wall.

- **Cavity:** the foot bottom offset 0.7 mm along the normal, with one plane over the flat. Seated normal gap is 0.7-0.8 mm: exactly 0.7 on the curve, and up to 0.1 more on the flat, which copies the foot's own print waviness. A cavity slightly thinner than tube plus glue presses the slopes harder than the flat, which is where the rubber has to be held.
- **No rim stop:** the cavity ends at the plane z = 3.8 mm. The rim stays 0.7 mm from the foot, so the tube is not pinched there and the excess leaves over the open top.
- **Glue line:** dry, without the tube, the foot lands on the flat floor 0.7 mm lower. A pencil run flat on the mold top then marks the 4.5 mm line on the foot. There the side wall is about 75 deg from the floor, so the edge sits on the side; at 3.0 mm it sat on a 55 deg slope. 6.0 mm would need 6.7 mm of dart per corner. Sim floor contacts reach 1.6 mm.
- **Lid:** its underside is the top of the slip-fit foot lowered 0.1 mm wherever that top is above 12.0 mm. That covers the rim and the toe block, about 901 mm2, right over the curved edge. Fins narrower than 2.4 mm are closed off, because they failed the 1.2 mm wall check. Four M2 x 10 self-tapping screws go through 2.4 mm holes into 1.6 mm pilots in the corner posts, 6 mm deep. One heel post sits 8 mm in from its corner (+y on the left mold, -y on the right), so each lid fits only its own mold and only one way round; L or R is engraved in the lid top. With the lid down on the posts, the foot sits 0.1 mm below the nominal seat, so the rubber is always pressed.
- **Checks** (`press-mold-check.json`): no overlap between the seated foot and the mold, or between the foot and the lid. Every STL reads back closed the way the release pipeline reads it.

Process:
1. Take the foot off the robot: undo the toe M2 x 6 and slide the foot off the bracket.
2. Dry fit the foot in its mold. The raised L or R is at the heel end. With a pencil flat on the mold top, draw the glue line around the foot.
3. Cut the blank: a rectangle about 62 x 50 mm from the slit-open tube, with the moulded seam and the splice kept out of it. Cut one straight slit at each corner, from the rectangle corner towards the foot corner, ending where the flat bottom of the foot turns into the curve. Remove no rubber. Dry, with no glue, wrap the blank on the foot, smooth inner face to the foot, curl across the foot width, and hold it with a rubber band or tape. The two sides of each slit now overlap; cut through both layers along the middle of the overlap with one knife stroke and lift out the two offcuts (a double cut), so the sides butt exactly. The excess per corner grows from 0.2 mm at 1 mm up the curve to 2.5 mm at 4.5 mm, which no straight V matches: the old `sole_template.pdf` darts (2.0 mm at a 2.5 mm wrap, where the excess is 0.87 mm) opened 1.1 mm gaps (Shawn's photo IMG_7134, 2026-10-24).
4. Unwrap. Clean both faces with dish soap and water, dry them, and wipe with alcohol. If no sandpaper is at hand, scrape the smooth inner face, the glue face, lightly with an upright knife blade.
5. Spread a thin E6000 film on the foot bottom up to the pencil line, and only there. The tube stays dry, so the flap above the line does not bond and comes away at step 9; the mold supplies the pressure that a two-sided contact bond would. Wait 2-3 minutes until the film is tacky.
6. Lay the tube smooth inner face to the foot, ribbed outer face out, as in the dry fit. The tube's own curl then helps the long sides round the foot; the 0.1 mm ribs on the floor face are moulding lines. Press the middle down, then work the edges over the curve so the cut edges at each corner butt.
7. Set the foot in the mold. Put the lid on and drive the four screws in turns, crosswise, until the lid sits on all four posts.
8. Leave it for 48-72 h at room temperature, because E6000 cures slowly below 15 C. Unscrew the lid and lift the foot out.
9. Lift the unglued flap above the glue line and cut it off with scissors along the bonded edge.
10. Refit the foot to the robot.
